At these ranges the swarm is mostly disconnected in flight (direct link range is a few hundred metres under the RF model), so first-attempt delivery is low everywhere. Store-and-forward is what turns a mostly-offline swarm into a working one: alerts wait in the QoS-1 buffer and flush when the UAV returns to base. The old instant relay (a UAV geometrically between the endpoints) adds nothing because that rarely holds in a sparse swarm. Forwarding replaces it: any UAV that receives an alert keeps it and hands it over whenever it later meets a recipient, so no UAV has to be in the right place at the right time.

5   What these results do and do not show

  • Simulation, not flight. Dynamics are kinematic (constant speed, linear battery drain). The EW-CVT, hysteresis, failover and DTN logic are real code under test; the aircraft and the radio are models.
  • Perception inside the mission replays static frames. Each sector shows one dataset image, so detection results are per-frame and deterministic; mission-level precision/recall mainly reflect the detector on those frames plus sector coverage.
  • Network assumptions. Log-distance path loss (exponent 2.5, σ = 4 dB shadowing), 33 dBm at 2.4 GHz for arenas ≥ 2 km. Results depend on this model; real links would differ.
  • Energy rule. A UAV at ≤ 15 % battery aborts and returns to base; at 0 % it is lost. The battery-sensor noise levels (0–4 % points) are scenario assumptions and are swept above.
  • Dynamic re-planning is not free. In the nominal case the static baseline finishes faster; re-planning buys resilience (failover) and energy balance at a time cost, shown in the headline table.
  • Statistics. 20 seeds per variant; ± values are standard deviations in tables and paired 95 % confidence intervals in the headline table. Seeds vary the world layout and launch batteries only.
